# Welcome to Quran Life

> **Branch note (daily-portion-only + Anki):** This guide is updated for the 3-tab workflow on this branch - **Daily Portion** (passive), **Anki Deck** (active memorization + splits + mindmaps + export), and **Documentation**. Reviews that were previously in-app **Practice/Smart Reviews** now happen in **Anki** with manual flag/tag handling. See each phase note below.

Quran Life is more than just a tracking app—it's a complete system for memorizing the Quran using visual memory and smart scheduling. This guide will take you from your first day to mastery.

## Phase 1: Setting Up Your Territory

When you first launch the app, you'll go through an **Onboarding** process to set your initial preferences. If you ever need to adjust them, you can do so in the [Settings](/docs/philosophy/settings) tab:

1.  **Set Your Active Part**: Choose the Part (Juz) you are working on. This focuses your Daily Portion and overall progression on your current goal, while Practice focuses the active preparation and repair queues on the same daily workflow. Quran Life currently uses a [7-part system](/docs/mindmaps/part-0) for active focus.
2.  **Skipping Surahs**: You can mark Surahs you have already fully mastered (like Surah Al-Fatiha or Al-Ikhlas) as **Skipped**. This removes them from your daily portion, preparation queue, and reviews to prevent cluttering your workflow with things you already know perfectly.
3. **Setting surah maturity**: You can change the maturity of a surah in the [settings](/docs/philosophy/settings), if you already know that it is strongly embeded in your memory. This will change the behavior of the relevant review items that are talked about in phase 4 below.

## Phase 2: The Passive memorization (Daily portion)

Every day, open the **Practice** tab. Your first goal is **Passive Exposure**. This ensures that you maintain a constant relationship with the entire text you are memorizing.

-   **Listen/Read Your Portion**: This is a specific set of verses the app gives you daily. Listen to it to subconsciously absorb the verses.
-   **How it's Calculated**: Daily portion only includes surahs in your active part that are still in passive exposure. Skipped surahs are excluded, and a surah also leaves daily portion as soon as any of its verse groups has entered review. If you later reset that surah's verse-group memory nodes back to fresh/new state in [Settings](/docs/philosophy/settings), it can appear in daily portion again. After eligibility is decided, the app sizes the portion around your chosen **daily minutes** and then derives the cycle length from whatever content is still eligible.
-   **Goal**: The intent is to keep your daily time steady while the number of cycle days adjusts to the amount of material still in passive exposure.
-   **Hafs Mushaf Standard**: We use the official **Hafs Mushaf** text to ensure your memorization matches the most widely used standard.
-   **The Dot**: A small dot on the "Practice" icon will remind you if you haven't finished your daily portion yet.

It is recommended to move on to the **active learning phase** (marking it as complete for reviews) once you have completed enough Daily Portion **cycles** to feel the "sound" and general flow of the verses. Once you meet this criteria, you might need a tiny amount of rote repetition at the very end to bridge any final gaps before marking a Surah as fully learned. This should be quick and targeted. 

However, if you are in a rush, you can bypass the passive learning phase and use rote memorization to "cram" the verses into your short-term memory. Once you know the words, you **must still create or use pre-made mindmap** to lock them in.

## Phase 3: Active Memorization (Visual Mindmaps) - Now in Anki Deck Tab

This is where the real work happens - now in the **Anki Deck** tab, not the old **Practice** preparation queue. Instead of just repeating verses, you will associate them with a visual "map."

1.  **Choose Your Path**: For every Surah, you have two options:
    -   **Use a Pre-Made Map**: Start with the official mindmap for speed and reference (as of now only part 7 mindmaps are available).
    -   **Edit/Create Your Own**: In the **Anki Deck** tab, use **Edit Mindmap** to open the full tldraw editor (same as main branch) and build your own map. This usually gives stronger memory because you make the structural decisions yourself.
2.  **Setup Your Verse Groups**: In the **Anki Deck** tab, use **Splits** (Add anchor / Edit Splits) to split the Surah into verse groups. For short surahs ≤10 verses you get an auto single group. The card preview shows how chunks will be cut by stop marks or max 10 words.
3.  **Export to Anki**: Once your map is ready and you feel familiar with the verses through your **Daily Portion** cycles, click **Export** (single surah or full deck) and import the `.apkg` into Anki. Only now does the verse appear in Anki's review queue. This is necessary because without a visual map, your brain has no "place" to store the verses. The map provides the structure that holds the verses together.

> On this branch, mindmap + splits are edited in the **Anki Deck** tab using the same `MindmapEditor` + `SplitsModal` from main. The Anki card then shows a static preview; edit again in the tab and re-export to update.

*Deep Dive: [How to create effective Mindmaps](/docs/philosophy/mindmap-strategy)*

## Phase 4: Long-Term Mastery (Now in Anki)

Once you've "learned" a Surah, **Anki** will ask you to review it periodically, not the app.

-   **When to Review**: Anki uses **FSRS** (same algorithm as the app, see [Smart Review System](/docs/philosophy/spaced-repetition)) to show you verse groups just as you're about to forget them. Enable FSRS in Deck Options, Desired Retention 0.90.
-   **Review UI**: Cards show blurred chunks - click **Reveal** step by step (same stop-mark logic). Grading is via Anki buttons: **Remembered/Good**, **Not Remembered/Again**, **Postpone/Bury**. See Spaced Repetition doc for shortcuts.

## Maintenance: Manual Handling (No Auto Remediation Queue)

As you progress, there is no automatic **Practice** remediation queue on this branch. You handle it by hand in Anki:

-   **Memory Gaps**: If you fail a card 3 times, Anki's **Leech** tags it (`leech` tag, threshold 3 Tag Only) instead of the app auto-suspending. Or you manually add **Flag Red**. Open the mindmap in the **Anki Deck** tab and fix it, then re-export.
-   **Similar Verses (Mutashabihat)**: Cards carry tag `mutashabihat` and field `RelatedGroups` listing all related groups (no word-range filter). You judge if a fail was similarity-related and flag it yourself. See [Similar Verses](/docs/philosophy/mutashabihat).

> You can track your Daily Portion still in the app, but verse maturity and review forecast are now in **Anki Stats** / **Anki Deck** preview. See [Statistics](/docs/philosophy/statistics).
