# Mindmap-Driven Memorization

Mindmaps are the cornerstone of the Quran Life memorization strategy. They bridge the gap between abstract text and spatial memory.

## Why Mindmaps?

The brain is naturally wired to remember **places and spatial relationships** much better than abstract lists of text. By creating a mindmap for a Surah or a Part, you are:
- **Creating Visual Anchors**: Each "chunk" of verses is tied to a specific location on a canvas.
- **Structural Encoding**: You see the hierarchy and flow of themes within a Surah.
- **Personalized Meaning**: The act of drawing or arranging the mindmap yourself creates a stronger mental bond with the verses.

## The Daily Portion

Every day, you are assigned a specific portion of the Quran to listen to and read. This ensures that you maintain a constant relationship with the entire text you are memorizing.

- **Calculation**: Your portion size is determined by your **Active Part** and your **Target Completion Days** (set in Settings). We divide the total verses by your target days to get a baseline.
- **Intelligent Division**: The app automatically adjusts the end of your portion to avoid cutting off surahs mid-way, merging short trailing segments into the previous day's portion when it makes logical sense.
- **Goal**: The intent is to complete a full reading/listening cycle of your active territory every 7-30 days, depending on your preference.

## How it Works in the App

### 1. Two Levels of Mapping
Visual memory aids are key to long-term retention. We use two distinct levels:
- **Part Mindmap**: A high-level overview of an entire section (Part/Juz) to help you understand the core themes and flow between different Surahs.
- **Surah Mindmap**: A detailed visual guide for a specific Surah. You upload an image or use the built-in builder to split it into smaller, manageable segments for review.
- **Skipping Surahs**: If you are not ready to tackle a specific surah, you can "Skip" it in the Settings. A skipped surah will be removed from your Daily Portion and Todo list, allowing you to focus purely on your active goals without distraction.

### 2. Creation & Anchoring
In the **Todo** tab, you can add "anchors" (specific verse ranges) and use the integrated `tldraw` editor to design your map. Mark it as **Complete** to begin its review cycle.

#### Create or Import?
You have two main paths for your visual anchors:
- **Create Your Own**: Using the built-in editor to create your own mindmap provides the most powerful **encoding**. The effort of deciding where themes sit and how they connect creates deep neural pathways.
- **Import Curated Maps**: You can also import pre-made mindmaps. This is excellent for **speed** and serves as a high-quality reference.

### 3. Verse Chunks and the AnchorBuilder
The core of our strategy is the link between your visual map and the actual verses.
- **AnchorBuilder**: Use this tool in the **Todo** tab to create "Splits" (verse chunks). Simply scroll to the desired point between verses and click "Split Here". This is essential when creating a mindmap from scratch or when fleshing out a map to better encode memory gaps.
- **Groups and Chunks**: Mindmapping requires you to create groups or "chunks" within a Surah. 
- **The Flow**: The connection between these groups should be strong and logically sound in your mind. However, the "flow" inside a specific chunk of verses is often weaker and more prone to errors.
- **Review and Refinement**: The review process reveals these gaps. If a specific chunk is consistently error-prone, it's a sign that your mindmap needs more **fleshing out** or that the chunk should be split further. Conversely, if a chunk is easily remembered, your initial passive encoding was successful. This is why we provide mindmaps as **editable blocks**—they are living documents that should evolve as your memory deepens.

*Learn how these chunks are reviewed using [Spaced Repetition](/docs/philosophy/spaced-repetition).*

### 4. Finalizing the Portion
After completing a few cycles of your daily portion (passive listening and reading), you might find that a tiny amount of **rote memorization** is needed to bridge the final gaps. This should be a very quick process, done just before marking a Surah as fully learned.

*Struggling with similar-sounding verses? Check our guide on [Managing Mutashabihat](/docs/philosophy/mutashabihat).*

## Philosophy Tie-back
By using mindmaps, we reduce **cognitive friction** during recall. Instead of struggling to find the next verse in a vacuum, your brain "sees" where it sits on the map you created.
