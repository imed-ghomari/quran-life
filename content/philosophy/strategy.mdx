# Visual Memorization Strategy

Mindmaps are the heart of the Quran Life strategy. They bridge the gap between abstract text and your brain's powerful spatial memory.

## Why Visual Maps?

Your brain is naturally wired to remember **places and locations** much better than lists of text. By creating a visual map for a Surah, you are:
- **Creating Visual Anchors**: Each group of verses is tied to a specific spot on a canvas.
- **Seeing the Big Picture**: You see the themes and how they flow into each other.
- **Building Personal Connections**: The act of arranging the map yourself creates a stronger mental bond than just reading from a page.

## The Daily Portion (Passive Exposure)

Every day, you are assigned a specific portion of the Quran to listen to and read. This ensures that you maintain a constant relationship with the entire text you are memorizing.

- **Passive Learning**: Listen to your daily portion to subconsciously absorb the verses. This builds a foundation for your active memorization while you prepare your visual mindmaps.
- **How it's Calculated**: Your portion size is determined by your **Active Part** and your **Target Completion Days** (set in Settings). We divide the total verses by your target days to get a baseline.
- **Goal**: The intent is to complete a full reading/listening cycle of your active territory every 7-30 days.

## How it Works in the App

### 1. Two Levels of Mapping
Visual memory aids are key to long-term retention. We use two distinct levels:
- **Part Mindmap**: A high-level overview of an entire section (Part/Juz) to help you understand the core themes and flow between different Surahs.
- **Surah Mindmap**: A detailed visual guide for a specific Surah. You upload an image or use the built-in builder to split it into smaller, manageable segments for review.

> **Note on Surah Mindmap Limitations**: You might notice that current Surah mindmaps don't include every single logical detail or verse transition. This is by design. We limit the complexity for three reasons:
> 1.  **Overwhelming Complexity**: Mapping every logical connection is too big a task.
> 2.  **Retrievability**: A massive, cluttered map decreases your ability to quickly retrieve information ("less is more").
> 3.  **Efficiency**: Many transitions are obvious or already handled by other cues (like sound patterns or rhyme), so fleshing them out visually is unnecessary work.

### 2. From Listening to Mapping (The Transition)
- **The Shift**: You should only move on to the **active learning phase** (marking it as complete for reviews) once you have completed enough **Daily Portion** cycles to feel the "sound" and general flow of the verses.
- **Choose Your Path**: In the **Todo** tab, you can decide whether to **Import** a pre-made map for speed or **Create Your Own** for deeper memory encoding.

### 3. Splitting Verses (AnchorBuilder)
The core of the strategy is linking your visual map to the actual verses.
- **AnchorBuilder**: This tool is used **specifically when creating or editing a mindmap**. It allows you to create "Splits" (verse chunks) that correspond to the different sections of your map.
- **The Flow**: Your map should show the flow between different groups of verses. If you find yourself forgetting a specific group, it's a sign that your map needs more detail or that the group should be split into smaller pieces.
- **Editable Blocks**: Mindmaps are not set in stone—they should evolve as your memory deepens.

### 4. Final Polish
After listening to your daily portion for a few days, you might need a tiny amount of rote memorization (repetition) to bridge the final gaps. This should be very quick and done just before marking a Surah as fully learned.

## Why This Matters
By using visual maps, we reduce the mental effort needed for recall. Instead of struggling to find the next verse in a vacuum, your brain "sees" where it sits on the map you created.
