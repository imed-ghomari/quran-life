# Smart Review System (FSRS)

Quran Life uses the advanced **FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler)** algorithm to manage your review schedule. FSRS is a modern, data-driven system that outperforms older algorithms (like SM-2).

## How Your Brain Remembers

Memories fade over time. However, if you review information just as you are about to forget it, that memory becomes significantly stronger. FSRS calculates this "forgetting curve" precisely for every single verse.

*Our system uses [Visual Mindmaps](/docs/philosophy/mindmap-strategy) as the primary review unit to give your brain a physical 'place' to store these memories.*

## The Review Process

The smart system schedules reviews at optimal intervals to minimize your workload while maximizing retention.

- **Mindmaps First**: When you start a review, you will often see the **Mindmap** for that section first.
    -   *Why?* This primes your visual memory. Before you recite the specific words, you recall *where* they are on the map. This strengthens the connection between the location and the text.
- **Step-by-Step Revelation**: Long verses are shown word-by-word. This helps you check your recitation in small pieces, reducing the risk of missing minor errors.
- **Recall, Don't Read**: The goal is to recall the verse from memory *before* clicking to reveal it.
- **Grading Your Performance**: After revealing the verses, you have two simple choices:
    -   **Remembered**: You successfully recalled the verse. The system will increase the interval until you see it again.
    -   **Not Remembered**: You couldn't recall it or made a significant mistake. The system will show it again sooner to help you relearn it.
-   **Not Sure**: If you make a mistake but aren't sure if it was a genuine memory gap (e.g., an accidental click or distraction), use the **Not Sure** button. This postpones the review to a later time without resetting your progress entirely.

## Efficient Learning

FSRS is highly efficient. As you prove your mastery, you will see items much less often—eventually reaching a "Mastered" state where reviews occur years apart. This saves you hours of unnecessary review time in the long run.

## Why Reviews Sometimes Reset

Occasionally, a review interval might be shortened or reset. This happens to ensure your memory stays perfectly aligned with the app:
1. **Manual Changes**: If you mark a Surah as "To Learn" and then back to "Learned."
2. **Changing Verse Ranges**: If you change the start or end of a verse chunk. Because the "chunk" you memorized has changed, the app asks you to verify it again.
3. **Repeated Mistakes**: If you mark "Not Remembered" multiple times, the system will naturally show that item more frequently until you've mastered it again.

## Keyboard Shortcuts

For a faster and more focused review experience on desktop, you can use the following shortcuts:
- **Arrow Right**: Reveal the next verse chunk, or mark as **Remembered**.
- **Arrow Left**: Mark as **Not Sure** (Postpone).
- **Arrow Down**: Mark as **Not Remembered**.

## Why This Matters
Spaced repetition ensures **minimal wasted effort**. You don't spend time reviewing what you already know well, and you don't lose progress on what you've just learned. The app handles the "when", so you can focus on the "what".
