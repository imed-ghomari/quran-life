# Mindmap Strategy

Mindmaps are the heart of the Quran Life strategy. They bridge the gap between abstract text and your brain's powerful spatial memory.

## Why Visual Maps Matter
Your brain is naturally wired to remember **places and pictures** much better than lists of text. By using visual maps, we reduce the mental effort needed for recall. Instead of struggling to find the next verse in a vacuum, your brain "sees" where it sits on the map you created. This helps you:
- **Create Visual Anchors**: Tie each group of verses to a specific spot.
- **See the Big Picture**: Understand themes and how they flow into each other.
- **Build Personal Connections**: Create a stronger mental bond by arranging the map yourself.

## How it Works in the App

### 1. Two Levels of Mapping
Visual memory aids are key to long-term retention. We use two distinct levels:
- **Part Mindmap**: A high-level overview of an entire section (Part/Juz) to help you understand the core themes and flow between different Surahs.
- **Surah Mindmap**: A detailed visual guide for a specific Surah. You use the built-in editor to work with approved or existing mindmap content and split it into smaller, manageable segments for review.

> Mindmap editor policy: user uploads are not allowed. Do not upload any external content into the mindmap editor.

> Our Design Philosophy: The pre-made mindmaps are designed with a specific goal: to encode information for effective retention, not to serve as exhaustive tools for theological study. They prioritize simplicity, intuitiveness, and ease of recall over comprehensive detail. For this reason, they are not presented as the only or truest way to understand the Quran, but as tools primarily crafted to help you learn according to the rules outlined below. In line with this, current Surah mindmaps do not include every logical detail or verse transition. This is intentional: we limit complexity because (1) mapping every logical connection would be overwhelmingly complex, (2) overly large and cluttered maps reduce your ability to quickly retrieve information (“less is more”), and (3) many transitions are either obvious or already cued by features like sound patterns or rhyme, making additional visual representation unnecessary.

### 2. Splitting Verses
The core of the strategy is linking your visual map to the actual verses.
- **Split configuration modal**: This tool is used **specifically when creating or editing a mindmap**. It allows you to create "Splits" (verse chunks) that correspond to the different sections of your map.
- **The Flow**: Your map should show the flow between different groups of verses. If you find yourself forgetting a specific group, it's a sign that your map needs more detail or that the group should be split into smaller pieces.
- **Editable Blocks**: Mindmaps are not set in stone—they should evolve as your memory deepens.

## Rules for Effective Mindmap Creation

For those creating mindmaps from scratch or editing pre-made ones, following these rules ensures your map is a powerful memory tool, not just a drawing.

### 1. Ask the Right Question (The Art of Chunking)
The goal is to find the most appropriate grouping structure for the topic.
-   **Hypothesis of Chunking**: Create hypotheses until you find the best fit.
-   **Group Size**: Keep groups to **2 to 4 items**. If you have more, chunk further. Small chunks help solve problems and find patterns for better retention.
-   **The Core Questions**: To form these groups, ask:
    -   *"Why is this important?"* (Intuitive chunking)
    -   *"How are they related to each other?"*
-   **Follow Curiosity**: Let the order of your questions follow your natural curiosity rather than the linear order of the material. You will naturally move from the big picture to details.
-   **Emotional Connection**: Be curious, emotional, and personal when seeking answers. Good inquiry leads to good relationships, which leads to good chunking.

### 2. Work in Layers of Information
Build your map in three distinct levels:
1.  **Level 1: Logic (The Backbone)**: This is the main relationship or "cause → effect". It represents the flow or transition between verse groups.
2.  **Level 2: Concepts (How and What)**: This represents the actual verse group. It should mention the number of verses it covers.
3.  **Level 3: Important Details**: These are specific details that make the concept easier to understand. They should only be fleshed out *on demand* if the concept itself isn't clear enough.

### 3. Characteristics of a High-Quality Mindmap
A good mindmap is:
-   **Visual & Non-Verbal**: Use doodles and abstract concepts with as few words as possible.
-   **Processed & Focused**: It answers the specific question you started with ("aim") and prioritizes relationships that bridge concepts rather than just tree-like lists.
-   **Reflective & Freehand**: Drawn freely in an order that reflects *your* learning process and logical flow.
-   **Grouped & Intuitive**:
    -   Use sub-chunks if there are more than 4 branches.
    -   Determine hierarchy by asking "What's most important?".
    -   Link to prior knowledge.
-   **Directional & Emphasized**: Show clear flow of information (cause → effect) and make main concepts visually obvious (size, thickness).
-   **Efficient**: Designed to be retained and applied from recall. *Skew is okay*—it's normal for some parts to be denser than others.

## How to Effectively Review Mindmaps

When your mindmaps are due for review, use the **Teach Method** to solidify your understanding. The goal is to explain the map to yourself (or an imaginary student) in this order: **Big Picture → Concept → Big Picture**.

Follow these steps for a good explanation:

-   **Seek Big Picture Relevance**: Constantly ask yourself how this part fits into the whole.
-   **Reverse Explanation**: Explain the concept *first*, then give it a name. Don't just name it and then explain it.
-   **Flexible Teaching**: Don't get locked into teaching it the same way every time. Change your approach.
-   **Speak Out Loud**: This slows you down, allowing you to explore each point fully.

## Source Transparency for Pre-Made Mindmaps

For official pre-made mindmaps, each mindmap documentation page includes a **Source Verification** section maintained by the Quran Life team.

- It lists the references used to build the map.
- It includes exact page citations for each Surah/Part.
- It is for transparency and trust, not an extra task for learners.

If you want to create or edit a mindmap faster, you can use the linked sources directly. They are selected to support thematic (**mawdou'i**) interpretation rather than detailed analytical (**tahlili**) verse-by-verse interpretation.
