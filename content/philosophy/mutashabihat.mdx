# Managing Similar Verses (Mutashabihat)

**Similar Verses** (known technically as *Mutashabihat*) are verses that look or sound almost identical. They are one of the most common challenges in Quran memorization. Quran Life helps you tackle them head-on.

## Awareness During Review

To help during review, the app automatically identifies these verses and provide extra context—automatically showing more of the verse or giving hints—until the point where the similarity ends.

## How to Approach Similarities

You can handle similar verses in two main ways:

1. **Reactive (Errors during Review)**: This is the most efficient way. The app automatically flags similar verses in the **Practice** remediation queue only when you make a mistake on a verse with a known similarity during a review. This ensures you only solve problems when they actually arise or become relevant.

2. **Proactive Study**: If you want to be thorough, you can find a complete list of all similarities for your active part in the [Settings](/docs/philosophy/settings) tab.

## What the Similarity Count Means

When you see a similarity card in **Practice**, the number is meant to answer a simple question:

**How many comparison verses still need my attention right now?**

That is why the app does **not** count every raw match in the dataset. Instead, it focuses on the ones that are actually actionable for you at the moment:

- **Ready now**: If the matching verse belongs to a Surah you already learned well enough, it can appear in the count.
- **Not ready yet**: If that matching verse belongs to a Surah you have not reached yet, it stays out of the count for now.
- **Still open**: If you have not solved that distinction yet, it stays in the count.
- **Already handled**: If you marked it resolved or chose to ignore it, it drops out of the count.

So in practice, the Practice repair number reflects **only the unlocked comparison verses that are still unresolved**. This keeps the card focused on what you can meaningfully work on now, instead of overwhelming you with every theoretical similarity.

## The Best Way to Distinguish

When two verses are similar, it's tempting to use "tricks" (like remembering which letter comes first). However, we highly recommend using **logic**:
- **Thematic Flow**: Understand *why* a specific word is used based on the theme of the Surah. Logic is much harder to forget than a simple trick.
- **Visual Separation**: Place similar verses in different locations on your [Mindmap](/docs/philosophy/mindmap-strategy) to create a clear distinction in your mind.

## Why This Matters
By addressing similar verses explicitly, you remove the "second-guessing" that often happens during recitation. Instead of being unsure, you make a conscious decision on how to tell them apart.
