# Mindmap Strategy

Mindmaps are the heart of the Quran Life strategy. They bridge the gap between abstract text and your brain's powerful spatial memory.

> **Reading a pre-made mindmap?** Every map uses one shared set of symbols — check the [Mindmap Legend](../mindmaps/legend) first. Note: the downloadable pre-made collection is **still not ready**, so create your own maps for now.

## Why Visual Maps Matter
Your brain is naturally wired to remember **places and pictures** much better than lists of text. By using visual maps, we reduce the mental effort needed for recall. Instead of struggling to find the next verse in a vacuum, your brain "sees" where it sits on the map you created. This helps you:
- **Create Visual Landmarks**: Tie each group of verses to a specific spot.
- **See the Big Picture**: Understand themes and how they flow into each other.
- **Build Personal Connections**: Create a stronger mental bond by arranging the map yourself.

## How It Works in the App

### 1. Two Levels of Mapping
Visual memory aids are key to long-term retention. We use two distinct levels:
- **Part Mindmap**: A high-level overview of an entire section (Part/Juz) to help you understand the core themes and flow between different Surahs. The app currently uses a 7-part structure.
- **Surah Mindmap**: A detailed visual guide for a specific Surah. In the **Anki Deck** tab use **Edit Mindmap** / **View** to work with new or existing mindmap content and split it into smaller, manageable segments for review.
- **Part 0 Meta Mindmap**: This Part 0 meta mindmap summarizes relationships across Parts 1-7. It is a reference layer and does not change when you switch Active Part.

> Our Design Philosophy: These Qur’an mindmaps are designed primarily to help you remember, not to capture every detail or present the only way to understand the text. They intentionally favor clarity, structure, and ease of recall over completeness, limiting complexity so the diagrams don’t become dense and overwhelming. Many logical transitions are already communicated through the Qur’an’s rhythm, sound, and natural flow, so extra visual mapping is often unnecessary.

### 2. Splitting Verses
The core of the strategy is linking your visual map to the actual verses.
- **Splits in Anki Deck tab**: Use **Splits** section (Add anchor / Save) or the visual splitter. It allows you to create "Splits" (verse groups) that correspond to the different sections of your map.
- **Short-Surah default**: For Surahs with **10 verses or fewer**, the export automatically treats the Surah as one verse group, even if you did not save manual splits yet. If you save your own splits, your saved splits immediately replace that default single-group behavior.
- **The Flow**: Your map should show the flow between different groups of verses. If you find yourself forgetting a specific group, it's a sign that your map needs more detail or that the group should be split into smaller pieces, then **Save** and **Re-export** that surah.
- **Editable Blocks**: Mindmaps are not set in stone—they should evolve as your memory deepens. Edit in **Anki Deck** tab and re-export.

## Rules for Effective Mindmap Creation

For those creating mindmaps from scratch or editing pre-made ones, following these rules ensures your map is a powerful memory tool, not just a drawing.

### 1. Ask the Right Question (The Art of Chunking)
The goal is to find the most appropriate grouping structure for the topic.
-   **Hypothesis of Chunking**: Create hypotheses until you find the best fit.
-   **Group Size**: Keep groups to **2 to 4 items**. If you have more, chunk further. Small chunks help solve problems and find patterns for better retention.
-   **The Core Questions**: To form these groups, ask:
    -   *"Why is this important?"* (Intuitive chunking)
    -   *"How are they related to each other?"*
-   **Follow Curiosity**: Let your questions flow naturally from your curiosity. You will instinctively shift from the big picture to the details. However, it's important to recognize the text's superior order. Therefore, the mind map can be somewhat flexible in organization as long as it roughly follows the sequence presented by the material.
-   **Emotional Connection**: Be curious, emotional, and personal when seeking answers. Good inquiry leads to good relationships, which leads to good chunking.

### 2. Work in Layers of Information
Build your map in these distinct levels:
1. **Level 0: Big picture**: Every surah mind map should account for the part mind map it belongs to. This mainly relates to the surah's central theme, which should match the logic of the part mind map and ideally be unique.
2.  **Level 1: Logic (The Backbone):** This is the main relationship or "cause → effect." It shows the flow or transition between verse groups within the surah. An important point is that each transition always moves forward, not backward (like the ring theory), because each group must serve a unique purpose. Otherwise, the flow of memory is disrupted.
3.  **Level 2: Concepts (How and What)**: This represents the actual verse group. It should mention the number of verses it covers.
4.  **Level 3: Important Details**: These are specific details that make the concept easier to understand. They should only be fleshed out *on demand* if the concept itself isn't clear enough.

### 3. Draw well (Characteristics of a High-Quality Mindmap)
A good mindmap is:
-   **Visual & Non-Verbal**: Use doodles and abstract concepts with as few words as possible.
-   **Processed & Focused**: It answers the specific question you started with and prioritizes relationships that bridge concepts rather than just tree-like lists.
-   **Reflective & Freehand**: Drawn freely using your own hand writing, preferably for better encoding. However, other tools like the "arrow" or "text" tool can be used if you don't have the necessary device (drawing tablet or mobile tablet with pen support).
-   **Grouped & Intuitive**:
    -   Use sub-chunks if there are more than 4 branches.
    -   Determine hierarchy by asking "What's most important?".
    -   Link to prior knowledge.
-   **Directional & Emphasized**: Show clear flow of information (cause → effect) and make main concepts visually obvious (size, thickness).
-   **Efficient**: Designed to be retained and applied from recall. *Skew is okay*—it's normal for some parts to be denser than others.

## How to Effectively Review Mindmaps

When your mindmaps are due for review, use the **Teach Method** to solidify your understanding. The goal is to explain the map to yourself (or an imaginary student) in this order: **Big Picture → Concept → Big Picture**.

Follow these steps for a good explanation:

-   **Seek Big Picture Relevance**: Constantly ask yourself how this part fits into the whole.
-   **Reverse Explanation**: Explain the concept *first*, then give it a name. Don't just name it and then explain it.
-   **Flexible Teaching**: Don't get locked into teaching it the same way every time. Change your approach.
-   **Speak Out Loud**: This slows you down, allowing you to explore each point fully.
