Ignored, resolved, and custom mutashabihat: what to track in your own review

March 31, 2026 · 2 min read
Quran Life
Quran memorization companion

Not every mutashabihat issue deserves the same treatment.

Sometimes a similarity is not a real problem for you, even if it looks similar on paper. In that case, ignoring it is the honest choice. You are not saying the verses are unrelated. You are saying this is not an active confusion point in your memorization — so don't flag it, and don't clutter your map for it.

Other times it is a real problem, and then the goal is not just to label it but to resolve it. That means flagging the card, separating the two verses far apart on the mindmap, and adding the thematic distinction — until the confusion stops recurring. A resolved similarity is one you unflag and remove from your weekly check because it no longer fails.

And then there is one more important category: the similarities only you notice. Different users feel different pulls between verses. One person's mind may keep jumping between two places that another person would never confuse. When that happens to you, flag it and note the pair in your mindmap notes — your personal confusion points deserve the same treatment as the well-known ones.

So it's really about three attitudes together: some similarities should be ignored, some should be worked until resolved, and some need to be added by you because your own mind is showing a connection no list captured.

What to do with the surah that keeps producing the most mistakes

March 31, 2026 · One min read
Quran Life
Quran memorization companion

The surah with the most mistakes is not always your weakest surah in an absolute sense. Sometimes it is simply the one being stress-tested most honestly.

That is why error data should not be read as shame. It should be read as diagnosis. In practice that data is your Anki Browser: suspended leech cards, red flags, and failed reviews per Surah.

When one surah keeps surfacing, the answer is usually not "repeat the whole thing more." It is to ask what kind of weakness is showing up.

Is the problem sequence, mutashabihat, weak transitions, overloaded verse groups, or an underbuilt mindmap? Once the weakness is named, the fix becomes much more targeted — usually a mindmap edit plus a re-export, not more repetition.

Should you suspend only the mindmap card or the verse cards too?

March 31, 2026 · One min read
Quran Life
Quran memorization companion

In Anki you have two kinds of cards per Surah — the mindmap card and the verse-group cards — and you can suspend either kind independently in the Browser.

Suspending only the mindmap card makes sense when the structure still needs work but the verse groups themselves are trustworthy enough to keep reviewing. The map pauses; verse reviews continue.

Suspending both makes sense when you want structural work and verse work to stay tightly coupled — for example you are rebuilding the whole Surah map and don't want verse reviews running on a structure you no longer trust.

This is about honesty without waste. If part of your work is still valid, there is no reason to throw it away. But if verse review should not continue independently of the mindmap state, suspend both until the map is fixed.

Suspended, similarity, or mindmaps: what should you fix first each week?

March 31, 2026 · One min read
Quran Life
Quran memorization companion

Not every review signal carries the same urgency. Once a week, open the Anki Browser and triage in this order:

The simplest order is this: first do the mindmap work that gives you structure, then fix suspended verse groups, then resolve similarity cards.

Why this order? Because unresolved structure makes everything else weaker. Suspended verse groups come next because they point to a concrete break in review. Similarity cards are important too, but they usually become easier once the map and the verse stability are already in place.

So the rule is not complicated: build the structure, fix the broken review groups, then clean up the fine distinctions.

Why Quran Life hides the verse before showing it

March 31, 2026 · One min read
Quran Life
Quran memorization companion

The verse stays hidden at the start because if you see it too early, your brain switches from memory retrieval to recognition, and that feels easier than it really is. The Anki cards therefore protect the hard part of memorization: the moment where you must pull the ayah out of your own memory.

Then, once revealing begins, the card still does not show the verse as one giant block. It reveals it in smaller contextual chunks. Those chunks use waqf-aware breaks where possible so the meaning stays intact, and then apply a 10-word maximum when a chunk is still too long, because revealing too many words at once makes grading easier in the wrong way.

So when a long verse keeps failing, the chunk structure also shows you roughly where it breaks — use that to decide whether the verse group needs re-splitting or the mindmap needs a stronger landmark at that spot.

What review order should you choose, and when?

March 31, 2026 · One min read
Quran Life
Quran memorization companion

Review order changes the feel of your session — and since reviews happen in Anki, the order is controlled in Anki's Deck Options, not in the plugin.

In general, the sensible default is to leave Anki's order alone: due cards first, new cards mixed in as configured. That gives the best balance between urgency and flow, and it is where new users should start.

Change it only for a specific friction. Want to stay inside one Surah's flow for a session? Use a filtered deck or a Browser preview limited to that Surah's tag. Want mindmap cards separate from verse work? Study the mindmap cards on their own day.

So the rule is simple: keep Anki's default order unless you feel a specific friction — then scope a session with a search or a filtered deck instead of reshuffling everything.
